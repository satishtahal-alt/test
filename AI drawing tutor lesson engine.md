# Derive the lesson, don't generate it

An AI-assisted drawing tutor with a style-aware lesson engine is feasible today, and the most practical route is not a generative one. Every stage of a lesson (construction shapes, contour, tone, hatching, finish) should be derived deterministically from one analysis of one image and stored as a single editable drawing model; stages are then views of that model, so proportions cannot drift between steps. This is the design that the strongest published precedents used (DrawMyPhoto, The Drawing Assistant, How2Sketch), all of which are non-generative, and it is the only design that satisfies "useful without an LLM at runtime", zero marginal cost per lesson, reproducible lessons for teachers and photos that never leave the device. Generative image models are documented by their own literature as temporally inconsistent across steps, and vision-language models agree poorly with human graders on hand-drawn learner work (kappa 0.20 in one 2025 study), so both belong in optional, authoring-time roles only. The commercial field is open: products with reach sell hand-authored libraries, and the four photo-to-tutorial apps found are tiny and mostly portrait-only. The one real technical unknown is whether a classical pipeline can decompose an arbitrary subject into construction shapes a teacher would accept; no tool or validated recipe for that was found, so it is the first thing to test. Nothing in this report was installed, run or benchmarked, so every speed, quality and hardware statement is an estimate until the proposed spike measures it.

**How to read this report.** Three kinds of statement are kept apart throughout. *Confirmed* means a researcher read it from a cited source in this session; *estimate* means a number or judgement nobody measured; *recommendation* means a design choice proposed here. Verification caveats from the research are carried into the text where they apply, and the most important are gathered in section 8. One process note: the five researcher notes files were not present in the notes folder when this report was written (their saves had failed), so their content was recovered from the research session's own records and used together with the image-analysis notes supplied directly. The content is the researchers' own; nothing was re-researched.

## 1. Executive recommendation — feasible, with a deterministic core and optional AI

**Feasibility verdict: yes, for a deterministic-first product; not yet for a "generate the whole tutorial" product.** The evidence for the first half is direct. DrawMyPhoto turned a user's photo into separate line-work and shading steps by image processing and novices drew significantly better than with a grid method on blind expert ratings ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-json/wp/v2/msr-research-item/613377)). The Drawing Assistant extracted construction lines from an arbitrary photograph and gave registration-based corrective feedback ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00840853)). How2Sketch computed ordered scaffolds of geometric primitives with anchoring guidelines ([UCL](https://geometry.cs.ucl.ac.uk/projects/2017/how2sketch)). None used a generative model. The evidence against the second half is equally direct: a 2025 paper describes prior process-generation methods as showing temporal discontinuities and structural jumps ([Loomis Painter](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17344v2)), and no benchmark was found that measures step-to-step structural consistency of any current image model on how-to-draw sequences.

**The recommended approach has five parts.** First, a canonical drawing model: one table of elements with stable IDs (primitives, axes, landmarks, proportion guides, contours at nested levels of detail, tonal regions, texture regions), each carrying provenance, confidence and visibility. Second, stages as pure queries over that model plus a style, so a stage never owns geometry. Third, a style as a parameter vector that drives both how marks are rendered and which stages appear in which order. Fourth, a provider interface for every analysis capability with a mandatory deterministic implementation and optional local-ML and cloud upgrades. Fifth, measurement-based, descriptive feedback that reports match-to-guide and personal drill trends and never grades artistic quality.

**What to build first.** A web-technology core (TypeScript, SVG and Canvas) owning the model, stage derivation and rendering; a thin desktop shell; a replaceable local analysis sidecar using permissively licensed classical computer vision; a small hand-authored template library for subjects chosen without a photo; templated lesson text. This is the smallest architecture that validates the product, and it needs no model download, no account and no network.

**What not to do.** Do not train a large model. Do not generate each stage as a separate image. Do not ship a style preset named after a living artist. Do not let a language model produce geometry or scores. Do not treat a pencil-sketch filter as a lesson: stylisation changes how a picture looks, instruction changes the order in which a learner makes marks and why.

**Confidence.** High that the architecture is sound and licence-clean, because each piece has a precedent and a permissive implementation. Moderate that automatic construction shapes will be good enough without teacher correction on organic subjects. Low on all timing figures, because none was measured.

## 2. Competitive landscape — incumbents sell libraries, generators are tiny and nobody serves teachers

No app was installed or tested; product facts come from store listings and third-party trackers, some of which gate or summarise data.

**Every product with reach relies on hand-authored lessons.** Simply Draw is the largest found, at 4.7 stars from roughly 559,800 App Store ratings with in-app purchases reported between $8.99 and $119.99 ([App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1639875485)). ShadowDraw, an iPad and Apple Pencil app twice featured by Apple, lists $7.99 per month or $49.99 per year yet shows only 1,784 US ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1223507083); [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/shadowdraw-learn-how-to-draw/1223507083?country=us)). Art for Kids Hub charges $5.99 per month or $64.99 per year for follow-along video lessons ([Art for Kids Hub](https://artforkidshub.com/join-today/)). Drawabox gives its exercise-based lessons away and sells human critique ([Drawabox](https://drawabox.com/)); Proko sells individual courses with no subscription ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/are-subscriptions-a-thing)). The lesson for a new entrant is that lesson production is the incumbents' cost bottleneck and that reach follows distribution and device availability more than lesson craft: the iPad-only product has a small fraction of the phone-friendly product's audience.

**AR apps trace; they do not teach construction.** Sketchar is subscription-only at $7.99 per week, $14.99 per month or $69.99 per year ([Similarweb capture](https://similarweb.com/ja/app/apple/1221482822)); Da Vinci Eye is reported as a one-off $19.99 on Android by a retailer blog, which should be checked on the store ([KraftGeek](https://kraftgeek.com/blogs/artist-guide/the-top-3-ar-projector-apps-to-master-your-sketching-skills)). A long tail of 2025–26 clones borrows the "Simply Draw" name, a store-search confusion risk. Tracing is a different job from learning to build a drawing, though "print or overlay this step" is a reasonable bridge feature.

**The most common sourced complaint is the paywall, not the lesson.** Reviews of Simply Draw centre on paywalls appearing mid-lesson and thin free content; the analysis source is a vendor's sales teaser and is indicative only ([Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-simply-draw-app-user-feedback-analysis-app-store-us-152285)). Recommendation: one complete lesson free, and no interruption inside a lesson.

**Photo-to-tutorial generators exist but have negligible traction.** ArtifyAI (iPhone, December 2025) builds a step-by-step tutorial from a photo with difficulty and time estimates; it had zero ratings and under 1,000 downloads per the tracker and does not disclose its technique ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/artifyai/6756684175)). Sketch Tutor, a web app, converts a photo to a pencil sketch split into stages with ruler and guide overlays, is optimised for portraits and drew 4 Product Hunt upvotes ([hunted.space](https://www.hunted.space/product/sketch-tutor-image-to-sketch-tutorial)). Sketch Steps: AI Loomis Draw produces an 8-step head construction guide from a portrait, with coin or subscription metering, at 4.0 from 61 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763632360)). Draw AI generates a step guide and projects it for AR tracing ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755973252)). None shows multiple teaching styles, lesson editing or teacher and parent sharing, and review volume is too small to judge quality. Four independent launches in about a year validate the idea as obvious; nobody with distribution has executed it. Two of the four are portrait-only, which marks general-subject construction as the hard, differentiating problem. Sketch Tutor's maker publicly asks whether the stage count and guides are right for beginners, which argues for making step granularity and guide density adjustable.

**The research record favours classical pipelines and is honest about what was measured.** Beyond the three systems named in section 1: ShadowDraw (the 2011 research system, unrelated to the app) blended edge maps from a database under the user's strokes and produced better-proportioned quick drawings, with the significant gain concentrated in one skill group ([UT Austin](https://vision.cs.utexas.edu/projects/shadowdraw/shadowdraw.html)). iCanDraw chose faces because novices make common, detectable errors such as placing eyes too high ([UIST 2009](https://uist.acm.org/archive/adjunct/2009/pdf/posters/paper157.pdf)). PortraitSketch adjusted traced strokes in real time and found that assistance did not reduce users' sense of ownership ([Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/portraitsketch-face-sketching-assistance-for-novices)). SketchTivity scores precision, smoothness and speed, and its own authors call that feedback insufficiently actionable ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10418340-power-feedback-improving-sketchtivitys-feedback-system)). These studies measured the quality of drawings made while assisted and self-reported understanding. No retrieved study shows durable skill transfer after the tool is removed, so marketing should claim "helps you complete a better drawing and understand the order of work", not proven skill gains. Two caveats travel with these citations: The Drawing Assistant's full text could not be retrieved, so its participant counts and conditions are unknown, and DrawMyPhoto's author list was not confirmed by the page retrieved.

**Teacher tooling is an apparent gap.** Searches for tools that let art teachers author, edit and share step sequences returned only consumer apps with fixed libraries; the search was shallow and teacher marketplaces and classroom platforms were not examined. How2Sketch's stated motivation was that expert-made tutorials are scarce and costly ([UCL](https://geometry.cs.ucl.ac.uk/projects/2017/how2sketch)). A deterministic lesson is a small data file that reproduces identically, which makes "generate, edit, print or project, share by link" a natural teacher feature.

**Not verified.** Quick, Draw!, AutoDraw, Procreate, Adobe Fresco and Firefly, Canva, Skillshare, Domestika, New Masters Academy, 21 Draw, Ctrl+Paint, Tayasui Sketches, Crayola apps and Drawing Desk were not checked this session; the working assumption that none generates lessons is background knowledge only. The current status of Osmo's Masterpiece app after the Byju's bankruptcy is unconfirmed.

## 4. Artist and pedagogy analysis — a loose style measured from 56 drawings, a structured order from catalogue records

### Attribution, and what the image evidence can and cannot support

**The brief names the artist as "Sean Biggs"; the X account at the supplied URLs displays "Sean Briggs" (@Sean_Briggs).** This report records the discrepancy and draws no conclusion about identity. Forty-one of the 57 local files carry a handwritten signature in which the two spellings cannot be told apart at this resolution. An Artfinder profile under the Briggs spelling describes a UK illustrator working in graphite, watercolour and ink with digital finishing ([Artfinder](https://www.artfinder.com/artist/sean-briggs/about)); it was not verified that this profile and the X account belong to the same person.

Four limits govern everything that follows. The text of all 14 X posts was read without logging in and contains captions and facts about the subjects, with nothing about drawing method; the attached image was seen for only 2 of the 14 posts, so **the X post images were not examined** and the analysis rests on the 57 local files. Those files are all 512×512, so stroke counts, lengths and spacings are visual estimates and **every numeric parameter range below is an estimate to tune**; only the tonal fractions were measured, by script on a downsampled copy, to about ±0.03. The set contains finished drawings only, with no process or teaching sheets, so **any stage order is a recommendation designed to reach the observed end state, not the artist's observed method**. And one file duplicates another, leaving 56 unique drawings.

Consistent with the legal findings in section 8, the product should not present this as a named-artist preset. The images are used here as evidence for a generic, technique-named style: **loose expressive sketch**.

### What the images show

The set is entirely monochrome, single-subject and on white: 20 animals, 15 buildings or architectural details, 14 plants, 7 vehicle files and one figure. There are no still-life objects, portraits, hands, interiors or developed skies, so the set says nothing about those subjects.

**Blank paper is the dominant feature, and it was measured.** The blank fraction ranges from 0.35 to 0.86 with a median near 0.75; 40 of 56 drawings are at least 70% blank, and the five below 60% are all dense architecture or machinery. The dark fraction has a median near 0.037 and is at or below 0.05 in 39 of 56. The typical recipe is one small dark cluster, one adjacent mid-tone mass and paper everywhere else, giving three value groups in effect.

**Contours are incomplete by design.** Closed outlines are the exception. In about 15 of 20 animal images the light-side contour is a single hairline or absent, and subjects trail off at the neck, base or far side. Continuous outlines appear on hard, man-made or hard-shelled forms. The heaviest marks are short and clustered at eyes, nostrils, openings and under-edges; the long contour lines are among the lightest marks on the page. The rule that fits the animal set is to draw the outline only where neighbouring tone does not already show the edge.

**There is no visible construction.** Explicit geometric guides appear in 0 of 57 files, yet wheel ellipses, tapering towers and arch symmetry hold. Pale searching lines survive in about 15 drawings, and lines overshoot corners in seven architectural pieces. Accurate proportion without a visible scaffold means the scaffold was erased, hidden, traced or unneeded, and the images cannot say which. A tutor therefore has to supply the construction stage itself. The surviving faint lines suggest one teachable habit: keep the guide at low contrast and do not ink over all of it.

**Texture comes from switching mark families, not from wrapping hatch around form.** Fur around heads follows growth direction, but body fur is often a single diagonal hatch that ignores it. Masonry, planks, feathers and tyres get grain-following strokes; vegetation gets strokes swept one way; soft bodies and cast shadows get a default diagonal. Teaching "always wrap hatching around the form" would contradict roughly a quarter of the animal images. Cross-hatching is clear in about 11 drawings; true stipple is rare.

**Economy is an area budget, not a stroke budget.** The sparsest drawings hold roughly 100–400 visible marks and the densest well over a thousand, so "few strokes" is not a property of the set. Roughly 5–15% of the subject's area receives most of the fine marks: the eye and face for animals, openings and rooflines for buildings, the mechanical core for vehicles. A stroke-count metric alone would mis-score the dense pieces.

**Backgrounds are omitted.** A full-frame background appears in 0 of 57; 37 of 56 have a ground anchor (a shadow patch, grass strip or ground line) that is wider than tall and fades laterally.

**One preset cannot fit all subjects.** Blank paper, a small focal dark, peripheral dissolve, material-specific marks and a ground anchor hold across animals, plants, buildings and vehicles. Contour continuity, stroke straightness and density do not. The evidence supports at least an "organic" and a "built or mechanical" sub-preset. With a single human figure in the set, nothing generalises to people.

### Teachable operations for the loose style

The image analysis yields fifteen operations. The three that most separate this style from a conventional outlined, fully shaded sketch are broken contour, the focal dark cluster and peripheral dissolve, together with the blank-paper constraint. Stroke speed, pressure and taper are not recoverable from the files.

| Operation | Adjustable parameters (estimates) |
|---|---|
| Low-contrast placement line kept in the final | Opacity 0.15–0.35 of the darkest line; covers 0.2–0.6 of the silhouette |
| Restated contour with offset strokes | 2–3 repeats; offset 0.3–1% of width; on 10–30% of the contour |
| Broken contour where form is light against paper | Completeness 0.4–0.7 organic, 0.7–0.95 hard-surface; gaps 3–15% of silhouette length |
| Long gestural line for unresolved secondary form | 1–3 strokes; 20–60% of width; light |
| Focal dark cluster | 2–8% of frame (measured median dark 3.7%); layered short strokes |
| Mid-tone mass by single-direction hatch | Angle within about ±15°; spacing 0.5–2% of width; 6–17% of frame |
| Cross-hatch layering for deeper tone | 2–3 directions; second layer rotated about 60–90°; paper still visible |
| Grain-following hatch | Direction locked to course, plank, feather or tyre |
| Gesture-swept strokes | One global direction with ±20° jitter; 5–25% of width |
| Unit sampling of repeated elements | 0.2–0.5 of units drawn; densest near the focal area, none at the periphery |
| Peripheral dissolve | Marks thin over the outer 20–40% of the subject |
| Ground anchor patch | Width 0.5–1.2× subject width; height under 15% of frame; fades at both ends |
| Horizon or depth hint | 1–4 long horizontals; optional |
| Overshoot at corners, built forms | 2–8% of line length; a minority of corners |
| Accent scatter | Optional |

**Recommended lesson steps for the loose style.** Place the subject with the lightest marks for top, bottom, sides and one tilt line, and leave them visible. Ghost the big shape two or three times without correcting. Choose one focal spot. Build the focal dark by layering short strokes until it is the darkest thing on the page, keeping a speck of paper inside it. Spread a mid-tone mass outward in one-direction hatch, stopping well before the edge. Add contour only where it is needed, with a hairline or a gap on the light side. Switch marks for each material. Sample repeated units near the focal spot and let them thin to nothing. Let the far end go with one or two long loose lines. Anchor to the ground with a short fading patch. Then run a stop check: squint, and if less than about two thirds of the page is still white, the drawing has gone too far. For built subjects, freehand the main verticals and horizontals after the ghosting step and let them run slightly past the corners. This sequence agrees with published loose-sketching teaching, which front-loads a fast structural pass and then teaches selection: Marc Taro Holmes's reviewers describe a pencil layout, a calligraphic ink line and then spotted blacks, with a "gradient of interest" ([Chris Wilson's review](https://chriswilsonstudio.com/the-urban-sketcher/)), and Liz Steel's course is organised around edges, shapes and volumes ([Liz Steel](https://www.lizsteel.com/foundations-friday-index/)). Those principles come from reviews and course indexes, not the books' text.

### The structured approach, from accessible Dunn resources

**What was accessible was narrow.** Alphonso Dunn's books and videos were not accessed. The evidence is library catalogue records, retailer blurbs and one independent review, most seen through search summaries. No statement about stroke direction, spacing or beginner mistakes can be attributed to him from this research.

The catalogue record for *Pen and Ink Drawing: A Simple Guide* (2015) lists nine chapters, running through supplies, strokes, shading, simulating texture, sketchbooking and the stages of a finished drawing ([Marmot record](https://bemis2.marmot.org/Record/.b60267756)). A second record gives sub-sections: pen control and stroke types before shading; seeing values, then value scales, then gradation; shading block, round and then complex forms; a texture reference chart, then "texture follows form", then texture on block, sphere and cylinder ([ICPL record](https://search.icpl.org/Record/1474904)). An independent review describes the companion workbook as one technique per page in a fixed pattern of brief instruction, reference example and the learner's own attempt compared with the example ([Parka Blogs](https://www.parkablogs.com/node/14111)).

The teaching order those records evidence is **control → value → form → texture → staged drawing**, with a drill, compare, apply loop. The concepts are long-standing pen-and-ink knowledge that predates him: an 1899 pen-drawing treatise is public domain in the United States ([Project Gutenberg #17502](https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/17502)), and mainstream instruction teaches cross-contour and cross-hatching independently ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-cross-contour-on-3d-forms)). What is his own is the selection, sequencing, naming and wording, his texture chart, his drills and his example drawings; an app must not copy those. The generic doctrine can be taught in original words and diagrams.

**Structured operations and their checks.** Each concept splits into a short lesson, a demonstration, a drill and one or two computable checks. All thresholds need tuning on real data; no validated thresholds were found.

| Concept | Drill | Computable check |
|---|---|---|
| Line control | Parallel-line rows, connect two dots, ellipses in boxes | Deviation from a fitted line, wobble, endpoint overshoot, strokes per target line |
| Parallel hatching | Swatches at set spacings | Angle variance in a patch, spacing regularity, hooked ends |
| Value scale and gradation | 5-, 7- and 9-step scales; gradient bar | Mean luminance per cell, monotonic order, evenness of steps, no banding |
| Cross-hatching | Four-layer swatch | Number of dominant orientations, inter-layer angle, per-layer spacing |
| Contour hatching | Hatch a sphere, a cylinder | Stroke tangents against the known direction field of the generated primitive |
| Light logic | Shade box, sphere, cylinder, cone | Two-cluster tone separation, shadow boundary position, cast shadow on the correct side |
| Stippling | Dot gradient, stippled sphere | Dot density against the target value map, clumping, dashes |
| Texture over form | Swatches, then texture on a cylinder | Texture energy should follow the value map rather than be uniform |
| Stages of a drawing | Staged subject drawing | Construction strokes before detail strokes, tone after contour coverage |

### Comparing the two approaches

| Dimension | Structured | Loose expressive |
|---|---|---|
| Role of construction | Explicit, visible, a stage of its own | Abbreviated; a light placement pass left partly visible |
| Tone and contour order | Contour, then tone | Tone grouping and the focal dark before contour refinement |
| Contour | Complete, then cleaned | Selective; dropped where tone already shows the edge |
| Hatching | Form-following, even spacing, layered | Mark family per material; default diagonal acceptable; abrupt transitions |
| Finish | A defined finishing stage | No finish stage; the terminal step is deciding to stop |
| What is practised | Control, value, form | Selection, focus, omission |
| What can be checked | Geometry, spacing, value steps | Constraint compliance: blank fraction, dark concentration, detail falloff |

The two are compatible, because loose teachers themselves teach a structural pass first. Vilppu's figure course interleaves gesture with spherical and box forms from the start ([Vilppu Academy](https://vilppuacademy.com/products/figure-drawing-1)), and Nicolaides pairs slow contour with fast gesture in the same schedule ([Cameron Art Museum](https://cameronartmuseum.org/classes_workshops/natural-way-to-draw-gesture-contour-modelled/)). The recommended combination is to **draw the same subject twice**: once through the structured stages with heavy support, then again under a constraint (two minutes, thirty strokes, two values, detail in one zone only). Selection then becomes a decision made against known structure rather than a way of hiding missing structure. No controlled study comparing structure-first with loose-first was found; this ordering rests on teacher practice and general instructional research.

### A style-control system that drives rendering and step order

A style is a named parameter set with two parts: render parameters and a stage template. Each control below changes both. The loose-sketch preset values come from the image analysis and are judgement calls, not calibrated.

| Control (0–1) | Effect on rendering | Effect on step order | Loose preset: base / organic / built |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line looseness | Seeded wobble, restatement, overshoot | High values add a "ghost the shape" step and remove a clean-up step | 0.75 / 0.85 / 0.60 |
| Contour continuity | Fraction of silhouette drawn; gap placement on the light side | Low values move contour after tone and make it conditional | 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.70 |
| Geometric simplification | Level of detail of the contour; angularity | High values end the lesson at a coarser level | 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.35 |
| Stroke economy | Mark and area budget | High values add a budget constraint and a stop check | 0.50 / 0.60 / 0.30 |
| Construction-guide visibility | Ghost opacity of guides in later stages | Zero inserts an erase step; high keeps guides to the end | 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.15 |
| Detail density | Share of secondary contours and units drawn | Adds or removes detail stages | 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.70 |
| Texture strength | Texture marks per region; mark family switching | Adds a "switch marks per material" step | 0.70 / 0.70 / 0.75 |
| Tonal contrast | Number of value groups and the darkest value | High values add a "build the focal dark" step early | 0.85 / 0.85 / 0.85 |
| Hatching direction (0 fixed angle, 1 form-following) | Direction field source | High values require a cross-contour planning step first | 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.65 |
| Hatching spacing (0 tight, 1 open) | Pitch per value band | — | 0.40 / 0.45 / 0.30 |
| Hatching layering | Number of crossed directions | Each layer is its own step when high | 0.55 / 0.45 / 0.75 |
| Sharp versus lost edges (1 all lost) | Edge softening and peripheral dissolve | High values add a "let the far end go" step | 0.60 / 0.70 / 0.40 |
| Background detail | Ground anchor only, up to a full setting | Above a threshold, adds a setting stage | 0.08 / 0.05 / 0.12 |
| Degree of finish | Where the stage list stops | Truncates the template | 0.50 / 0.40 / 0.70 |

Target outcome bands for the loose preset are a blank fraction of 0.68–0.84 organic or 0.45–0.75 built, and a dark fraction of 0.01–0.08 organic or 0.05–0.20 built.

**Generalising across subjects.** The model separates three things: a subject model (form decomposition, landmarks, value plan, texture zones), a style recipe (ordered stages) and a technique module (mark rules). Subject category selects a sub-preset and supplies direction fields. A bird is an egg, a sphere and a cone with hatch along feather axes and lights left open. A tree is a few clumped masses lit as spheres plus a cylinder trunk, with no outline in the loose style. A building is planes, one hatch direction per plane, with perspective as a prerequisite. An object such as a mug is a cylinder built from two ellipses. A landscape has no single subject mask, so the engine works from tonal masses and a horizon and the background control does the work a contour does elsewhere. The image evidence covers animals, plants, buildings and vehicles only; objects, faces, hands and landscapes rest on general pedagogy.

**Separate subject-reference and style-reference images.** Geometry comes only from the subject reference. The style reference contributes measurements, not pixels: blank and dark fractions, the number of value groups, an orientation histogram for hatch direction and layering, stroke-length mix, edge density falloff from a focal point and an estimate of contour completeness. Those measurements are mapped to the fourteen controls, shown to the user as an editable preset, and applied to the subject's own model. This keeps the lesson an instruction rather than a filter, and it works with no model at all. A diffusion-based preview that takes geometry from one image and look from another (SDXL with ControlNet and a style-only adapter) is possible on an 8 GB GPU, but its licences were confirmed only from secondary sources and prior knowledge, and it should be a cosmetic preview locked to the same line map, never the lesson itself. Style references should be the user's own work or generic examples, and presets must carry technique names.

### Curriculum modules, adaptive fading and honest assessment

**Curriculum.** Published curricula agree on a partial order, and the proposed module graph is a synthesis that no single source publishes and nobody has validated. Mark control comes first. It feeds flat shapes and then 3D forms (box, cylinder, sphere, cone, drawing through, cross-contour); proportion, sighting and negative space; and value control. Forms and value control together feed light and shadow; value control feeds the rendering techniques (pencil shading, hatching to cross-hatching to contour hatching, stippling); light plus one technique feeds texture and materials. Perspective follows forms and precedes buildings and vehicles. Animal construction precedes the figure, then the head and hands. A gesture strand runs in parallel from the first session. Composition and landscape need proportion and light. Expressive simplification comes after one full structured subject drawing. The constructive, perceptual, gestural and child shape-vocabulary families each imply a different first step, which is what should drive step order per style: for young children, one flat shape per step in the manner of the shape-alphabet tradition ([Brookes, described by a retailer](https://www.exodusbooks.com/drawing-with-children/brookes/2218/)); for perceptual work, edges, spaces, relationships, lights and shadows ([Edwards workshop curriculum](https://drawright.com/workshop-curriculum-overview)).

**Adaptive fading of assistance.** Worked examples help novices more than unguided problem solving, the benefit shrinks or reverses as expertise grows, and adaptive fading outperformed fixed fading in tutored problem solving ([Salden et al. 2010](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/the-expertise-reversal-effect-and-worked-examples-in-tutored-prob/)). That evidence comes from maths and science; its transfer to drawing is an assumption. The recommended ladder is trace, then ghost guide, then landmark dots only, then freehand, with a guide removed for a step once the learner's measured error on it has been repeatedly low, an always-available "show me" fallback, and a skip for experienced users. Tracing is defensible as a motor and confidence scaffold but appears to train something partly different from freehand drawing: in elementary pupils tracing and drawing performance were only weakly correlated ([Cohen et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0031512521990358)). Traced work should be labelled as traced.

**Honest progress assessment.** The app can measure six things: placement and proportion against the lesson's landmarks after a similarity alignment; contour coverage; tonal separation and agreement with the value plan; technique adherence (hatch presence, number of directions, spacing regularity); process adherence from timestamps; and drill metrics over time, which are the most trustworthy numbers. It must not claim to measure artistic quality, expressiveness, creativity or talent, and it should not give a whole drawing a percentage. The reasons are empirical. Vision-language models reading student drawings directly reached only kappa 0.20 against ground-truth scores, rising to 0.47 when given human descriptions ([Henkel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05538v1)); multimodal models lag humans on a benchmark of 1,015 hand-drawn student diagrams ([SketchJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06944v1)); and the authors of an art-scoring system state that reliability claims remain unanswered ([ArtMentor](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13832v1)). These figures are from 2025 and early 2026 and newer models may differ. A single accuracy number also conflates perceptual, motor and representational causes of error ([Chamberlain & Wagemans](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/20010/1/Chamberlain%20%26%20Wagemans%20-%20Errors%20in%20drawing%20pre%20formatting%20.pdf)).

For the loose style the checks change meaning. Deviation from the reference is intended, so contour-match metrics are widened or switched off and the app checks the style's constraints instead: blank fraction, dark-accent area and its concentration near the declared focal point, value-group count, detail falloff, ground anchor present and small, ink outside the subject near zero. It cannot judge whether the focal point is well chosen, whether lost edges still read, liveliness of line or when to stop beyond the crude blank-fraction check. All loose-style target bands come from 56 finished works by one hand at low resolution and need validation with learner data before any grading use. Stroke-level metrics need stylus capture; from a photo of paper only area, value and edge measures are reliable.

Feedback should be one or two observations per step, at task or process level, tied to a next action and never a verdict on the picture or the person. Feedback directed at the self carries little learning information and feedback can harm as well as help ([Hattie & Timperley](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/Hattie_Timperly_2007.pdf)). Human layers complete the picture: a self-assessment rubric in child-friendly wording, teacher or parent review with the ability to hide automated metrics, and a portfolio that repeats a fixed benchmark drawing at intervals and shows first against latest. Rewards need care, because preschoolers who already liked drawing and were promised a certificate later drew less in free play ([Lepper et al., summarised](https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=7775)); streaks should be optional, forgiving and off by default for children, which is a precaution and not an established finding. Every guided lesson should end with an open step (change one thing, add a setting, draw it again from memory); no controlled study was found showing that step-by-step drawing harms or helps creativity, so this is a design hedge.

## 5. Proposed drawing representation — one element table, stages as queries

**No existing format covers the need, but each solves a piece.** The recommendation is a custom JSON drawing model in a zip container. It borrows stable element IDs and per-element version counters from Excalidraw ([schema docs](https://docs.excalidraw.com/docs/codebase/json-schema)), per-point attribute arrays including a time offset from Blender's Grease Pencil ([architecture docs](https://developer.blender.org/docs/features/grease_pencil/architecture/)), the separation of raw ink from a semantic layer from Wacom's Universal Ink Model ([model docs](https://developer-docs.wacom.com/docs/specifications/uim/model/)), and the zip-with-manifest, PNG layers and mandatory thumbnail from OpenRaster ([file layout](https://openraster.org/baseline/file-layout-spec.html)). SVG is an export and render target, not the source of truth: a path carries one stroke width, and SVG cannot cleanly hold provenance, confidence or stage logic.

**Four layers make the hybrid.**

| Layer | Contents | Form | Editable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primitive and guide | Frame, axes, construction primitives (ellipse, box, line, cylinder as two ellipses), named landmarks, proportion guides, light direction | Parametric vector with IDs | Yes: the teacher's main handles |
| Contour | Primary and secondary contours with nested levels of detail; occlusion order | Polyline as canonical geometry, Bézier fit for display, per-point width | Yes |
| Semantic region | Tonal regions with a value index and hatch angle; texture regions with a detail level; part labels | Closed polygons with IDs | Yes |
| Raster | Reference photo, mask, depth and normal maps, continuous tone map, hatch direction field, thumbnails | PNG assets named by content hash | No: derived, bulky, replaceable |

The rule of thumb is that anything a later stage is constrained by is vector with an ID, and anything that only informs rendering is raster. For every raster asset the vector boundary derived from it is kept, so a teacher's edit never requires re-running computer vision. Coordinates are normalised so stages, exports and learner overlays are resolution-independent. Hatching strokes are not stored; they are generated at render time from region polygon, angle, spacing, density and a seed kept in the document, and stored explicitly only when a teacher hand-edits them. Stage text, stage visibility overrides and stroke order are editable too.

**Each stage builds on the previous one without regenerating anything.** Every element records the stage role at which it first appears, an optional role at which it retires, the construction elements it depends on, and its level of detail. A stage is a role, a filter, style overrides and an explanation. Rendering stage N draws all elements whose role comes at or before N, with earlier roles in a ghost style. The default roles are frame, masses, landmarks, primary contour, secondary contour, tone block, tone refinement and detail or texture; a style may reorder or drop them, which is how the loose style puts tone before contour. Three nesting properties make refinement additive:

- **Contours.** Simplification runs at three or four tolerances on one recursion tree, so the coarse vertex set is a subset of the finer one and coarse vertices keep their IDs. Refinement switches the level of detail on the same element instead of replacing the path.
- **Tone.** The three-value split is derived by splitting a class of the two-value split, so a shadow boundary at the block-in stage is still a boundary at the refinement stage. This is a design proposal; standard multi-level thresholding does not guarantee nesting.
- **Hatching.** Lines for the darkest level are generated once and each is assigned a tone threshold, so darker stages are lighter stages plus more strokes. This is the nesting property of tonal art maps ([Praun et al.](https://hhoppe.com/proj/hatching/)), a reference cited from prior knowledge and not re-fetched.

Fine contour points lying close to a construction primitive store a reference to it, so moving the primitive drags the dependent contour and every stage updates together. Published work supports the scaffold-first, coarse-to-fine default: How2Sketch for primitive scaffolds, an animated-construction system whose inferred stroke order a user study found comparable to an artist's ([Fu et al.](https://hongbofu.people.ust.hk/projects/animatedConstructionOfLineDrawings_SiggA11/index.html)), and the finding that artists' lines concentrate on occluding contours and strong intensity gradients ([Cole et al.](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Cole_2008_WDP/index.php)). The specific stroke-ordering sort keys (dependencies first, large to small, outside in, nearest next, mirrored for left-handers) are a proposal to validate.

**Uncertainty and hidden structure are first-class fields.** Each element carries a provenance source with five values: *observed* (measured from the photo or mask), *inferred* (computed by rule from observed data, such as a fitted ellipse), *generated* (produced by an ML or language model), *template* (from a subject template) and *teacher* (authored or corrected by a person). It also carries the provider and version that produced it, the inputs it derives from, a confidence between 0 and 1 whose meaning is documented per provider and not compared across providers, and a three-state visibility modelled on the COCO keypoint convention ([COCO format](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cocodataset/cocodataset.github.io/master/dataset/format-data.htm)): *visible*, *occluded* and *hypothetical*. A needs-review flag is set automatically when confidence is low, visibility is not visible or two providers disagree. Alternatives are kept, so the interface can offer "try the other guess" between, say, a box and an ellipse envelope. A teacher edit sets the source to teacher and locks the element against regeneration. On screen, solid lines mean observed or teacher-confirmed, dashed means inferred hidden structure drawn through the form, dotted and lighter means hypothetical; confidence numbers are never shown to child learners. No study was found on how learners read dashed against solid guides; the convention is borrowed from drafting.

**The line between reliable transformation and speculation is explicit.** Reliable: masks, landmarks, the depth and normals of the visible surface, relit shading of the visible surface, primitives fitted to visible silhouettes, and small parallax shifts. Speculative: the far rim of a mug, the back of an animal, any rotated view of a photographed subject, and textures of unseen regions. Single-image 3D methods invent unseen sides, and the literature documents the failure: one method struggles when the input lacks information "such as the back view of a fox" ([One-2-3-45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.16928)). A hidden bottom ellipse of a cylinder is therefore stored as inferred and occluded with a rule as its provider; a generated turnaround, if ever offered, is stored as generated and hypothetical and labelled an approximation. Learner strokes live in a separate layer with x, y, pressure and time per point and are never merged into the lesson model.

## 6. Recommended architecture — a TypeScript core, a replaceable analysis sidecar, AI at authoring time only

**Components and data flow.** A photo or a chosen subject enters through an ingest step that decodes, strips EXIF metadata, downscales and content-hashes it. An analysis chain produces a mask, contours, optional depth, tonal regions and a light estimate. A deterministic builder fits primitives, axes, landmarks and proportion guides, builds nested contours, attaches dependencies and assigns roles, provenance and confidence. The result is the canonical drawing model. From there a pure function derives stages from model plus style, and a second pure function renders them from stage list, render parameters and seeds to screen, SVG, a printable step sheet or stroke-by-stroke playback. A separate learner layer captures strokes or a rectified photo of paper and compares it in normalised coordinates. **Every step after the canonical model needs no machine learning and no network.**

**Which work is deterministic, which is ML and which is a language model's.**

| Task | Baseline (always present) | Optional local ML | Optional cloud, authoring time only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subject without a photo | Template library of hand-authored models | — | Structured generation into the same schema, then teacher review |
| Segmentation | User scribble or box with GrabCut | MobileSAM or SAM 2.1-tiny; BiRefNet-lite | Hosted segmentation |
| Edges and contours | Canny or XDoG, contour tracing, nested simplification | TEED | — |
| Primitives, axes, proportion guides | Moments, minimum rectangle, ellipse fit, hull, concavity cuts | — | — |
| Landmarks | Geometric extrema and teacher placement | MediaPipe face, hand, pose; rtmlib for animals | — |
| Depth, overlap, hatch direction | Part size and user reordering; structure tensor of luminance | Depth Anything V2-Small or DA3 Small/Base | — |
| Tone | Lightness thresholding into nested value regions | — | — |
| Light direction | User-set or fitted estimate, always adjustable | — | — |
| Stage derivation, ordering, rendering | Pure functions | — | — |
| Lesson text | Templates keyed by stage role, subject category and style, with slots filled from the model | Small local model rewrite | Rewrite, translation, age adaptation, cached in the document |
| Feedback on the learner's drawing | Geometric and tonal comparison with templated messages | — | Language-model phrasing of computed measurements only |

A language model never produces geometry or scores. That follows from the evidence on spatial reasoning: two 2026 benchmarks report significant limitations of vision-language models in depth perception and 3D geometry ([SpatiaLab](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/31ab8d228b704e86d9eb87f072d2f5dc-Abstract-Conference.html)). Light estimation from one image stays approximate and fails with multiple lights, so it is always presented as an adjustable suggestion.

**Modular model replacement and graceful degradation.** Each capability is a provider with an ID, version, tier, network flag, cost and latency estimate, availability check and run method. A resolver picks the highest permitted tier given offline mode, consent, child profile, device capability and a per-lesson budget; on error or timeout it falls to the next tier and records what actually ran. Every provider writes into the same schema, so downstream code never branches on which tier ran. Cache keys hash the input assets, provider, version and parameters. If no model is installed, the product still makes, edits, teaches, prints and exports lessons; if no photo is supplied, the template library covers common subjects; if analysis is poor, the teacher corrects handles on a model instead of re-prompting.

**Local versus cloud.** Local is the default and the baseline tier is the product. Estimated hardware envelopes, none benchmarked: a CPU-only laptop handles landmarks, small segmentation models, small depth models, classical edges, primitive fitting, rendering and templated text; an 8 GB GPU adds optional diffusion previews; nothing in the core needs more. Cloud is an opt-in enhancement behind a consent gate that states what is sent, to whom, why and for how long.

**Cost and latency.** The baseline has zero marginal cost per lesson. If hosted image generation were used for one reference and three to five stage images, the cost would be roughly $0.02–0.08 per lesson on cheap tiers and $0.50–1.30 on premium tiers; these are estimates derived from unit prices read on 10 October 2026, such as $0.014 per image for FLUX.2 klein 4B ([BFL pricing](https://docs.bfl.ai/quick_start/pricing)) and $0.134 for Gemini 3 Pro Image ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). No authoritative latency figure was found for any hosted API, and no CPU latency was measured for any local model. Proposed budgets, to be measured on a named reference machine: open a saved lesson in under 1 second, switch stage in under 100 milliseconds, and photo to model in under 5 seconds at about 1024 pixels on a mid-range laptop CPU.

**Licensing posture.** Use only MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, ISC or Boost components in the shipped product; treat GPL projects as ideas; keep every learned weight optional so the core has no weight or training-data risk. Before shipping any optional model, read its upstream LICENSE file, because several licences in section 3 were confirmed only through secondary pages. Avoid bundling ComfyUI, which is GPL-3.0; the in-process alternatives named in the research were not licence-checked this session.

**Cross-platform desktop and web path.** The path with least rework is a web-technology interface that owns the document model and stage derivation, wrapped in a thin desktop shell. Tauri 2 gives small installers, uses the operating system's webview and reached stable in October 2024 with iOS and Android targets ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/blog/tauri-2-0-0-beta/)); Electron bundles Chromium, giving uniform rendering at the cost of size. The choice should follow a one-week spike on stylus latency and canvas performance in each webview. Analysis starts as a local Python sidecar with OpenCV, scikit-image and Shapely, because that is the fastest way to prototype. It is the part that will not port to the web or to mobile, so it sits behind the provider interface; for the web build it is replaced by OpenCV.js and ONNX Runtime Web. Because analysis runs once per lesson and its output is the saved model, the sidecar is not needed to view, edit or teach a lesson. Current WebGPU availability in Safari, Firefox and the embedded webviews was not verified. Stylus input uses Pointer Events with pressure and tilt; paper capture detects the page quadrilateral, rectifies it and offers manual corner dragging as the fallback.

**Persistence and versioning.** A lesson is one zip file: a first-entry mimetype, a manifest with format version and an asset index, the model, an append-only edit history, content-addressed assets, a required thumbnail and optional pre-rendered stage SVGs so simple viewers work. The schema has an integer format version with ordered migration functions; readers refuse newer major versions but preserve unknown fields. Teacher edits are operations on ID-keyed elements, undo is the inverse operation, and regeneration never overwrites a teacher-authored or locked element. That operation log makes later collaboration through a CRDT library possible without redesign; none should be adopted in the prototype. SQLite as a file format is the alternative once lessons carry long attempt histories ([SQLite](https://sqlite.org/aff_short.html)). Exports are SVG per stage, a printable step sheet and stroke playback.

**Privacy for uploaded photos.** All analysis runs on the device by default. No account is needed to make or follow a lesson. EXIF is stripped before anything is stored. The photo lives only inside the lesson file, with a one-tap "remove photo, keep lesson" action, and sharing excludes the photo by default, since the vector model is enough to teach. No face embeddings or templates are computed or stored and faces are never matched; a landmark model used only to place guides runs locally and keeps only guide geometry. The UK regulator's guidance is that a photograph becomes biometric data when processed into a template used for identification ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/guidance-on-biometric-data/key-data-protection-concepts)); the reading that guide placement falls outside that is the researcher's, not legal advice. Hosted APIs are constrained by provider terms as well as by law: Google's Gemini API terms bar use in apps directed at or likely to be accessed by under-18s ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/terms)), and OpenAI's guidance says not to process personal data of children under 13 without zero data retention ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-checks/under-18-api-guidance)). A learner-facing drawing app plausibly falls inside the Gemini clause, which is a judgement for the product owner and reinforces the local-first design.

**Accessibility and age-appropriate design.** A handedness setting mirrors default hatch direction, demonstrations and panel side, and feedback tests consistency of direction instead of a fixed angle; right-handers naturally hatch on one diagonal and left-handers on the other ([Getty](https://www.getty.edu/news/leonardo-da-vinci-renaissance-lefty/)). Colour is never the only carrier of feedback, per WCAG 2.1 success criterion 1.4.1 ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color)); overlays differ in lightness and line style, and a greyscale preview doubles as a value-teaching tool. Step text is one short imperative instruction with read-aloud, adjustable size and spacing, and an off-white background, following dyslexia-friendly guidance ([Dyslexia Scotland](https://dyslexiascotland.org.uk/dyslexia-friendly-typed-formats)). For roughly ages 4–7: large targets, thick strokes, three to six steps, one shape per step, spoken instructions, no precision drills and no proportion scoring. A study of 2–5-year-olds found finger drawing on tablets helped younger children produce drawings ([PMC7069367](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7069367)); the researcher was not certain which of two URLs carried which finding. Stroke smoothing is off for line-control drills because it masks wobble, and an untimed mode exists. In a child profile there are no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs, sharing is adult-mediated and private by default, there is a parental gate before purchases and external links, and AI features are off. Motor-impairment, low-vision and screen-reader access were not researched.

**The smallest architecture that validates the product.** Schema version 1 and the zip container; five to ten hand-authored template models; the deterministic photo pipeline with user-assisted segmentation, nested contours, envelope and ellipse fitting and tonal regions; stage derivation and SVG rendering for one style; teacher edits to move, hide, lock and rewrite; templated text; step-sheet export; local processing only. No model download, no account, no language model.

## 7. Prototype specification — seven subjects, six stage types, measurable gates

**Scope.** In: the smallest architecture above, plus a second style so that the style system is exercised (structured construction pencil and loose expressive sketch), seeded hatching with nested tone, and a regression harness that computes the stability metrics. Out for the prototype: learner stroke capture and feedback, adaptive fading, any cloud provider, any language model, generated 3D, a sharing service, collaboration and mobile. One optional local model per capability may be trialled behind the provider interface to compare against the baseline, but the acceptance criteria apply to the baseline.

**Example workflows.**

- *A parent makes a lesson from a photo.* They photograph the family mug, scribble over it to isolate it, and get an eight-stage lesson. They drag the stage-count slider down to five for a seven-year-old, hide the proportion guide, print the step sheet.
- *A teacher prepares a class lesson with no photo.* They pick "house" from the template library, choose the hatching style, move one landmark that the whole lesson then follows, rewrite two explanations, and export a file that opens identically on every pupil's machine.
- *An adult learner explores style.* They upload a photo of their dog, follow the structured lesson, then switch the same model to the loose style and see the stage list reorder: placement, focal dark, mid-tone mass, selective contour, ground anchor, stop check.
- *A separate style reference.* They add a scan of their own sketch as a style reference; the app shows the measured preset (blank fraction, dark concentration, hatch directions) as editable sliders and applies it to the dog model.
- *Failure.* They upload a cluttered snapshot; the app reports that it could not isolate a subject with confidence, asks for a scribble or a crop, and never produces a lesson silently from a bad mask.

**Test set and stage types per subject.** "Primary" marks a stage type that must pass for that subject; "secondary" is run and reported; a dash means not tested in the prototype.

The face and hand tests use the optional landmark provider; their baseline without it is teacher-placed landmarks on a template. The expected quality ordering, an inference and not a measurement, is apple, mug and house good, the animal plausible but generic, the face dependent on landmarks, and the hand weakest.

## 8. Risks and unresolved questions — construction shapes, unvalidated thresholds and unread licences

### Technical

**Construction-shape quality is the central unknown.** No published, validated recipe was found for decomposing an arbitrary object mask into the shapes an artist would use; the skeleton-or-concavity proposal needs prototyping and visual evaluation. If it fails on organic subjects, the fallback is category templates driven by landmarks plus teacher correction, which narrows "any subject" to "common categories plus assisted authoring".

**Nothing was run.** Every repository's runtime status, every model's latency and every hardware tier is an estimate. Specific unknowns: TEED and small depth models on CPU; stylus latency in each webview; packaging a Python sidecar on Windows and macOS, including code signing and antivirus false positives; current WebGPU support in Safari, Firefox and embedded webviews.

**Smaller technical risks.** Photos of real scenes with clutter, cast shadows and patterned surfaces will break thresholding and segmentation more often than test images. Form-following hatching must be built in-house. Single-light estimation is approximate. Nested multi-level tone thresholds are a proposal that standard algorithms do not guarantee. Paper-photo assessment is weaker than stylus capture and depends on rectification and lighting. skeleton-tracing has not been maintained since 2022.

### Pedagogical

**No evidence of durable skill transfer was retrieved** for any drawing-assistance system; studies measured assisted drawing quality and self-report. No controlled study was found comparing structure-first with loose-first, tracing with freehand practice on later skill, or directed drawing with open-ended instruction on creativity. Adaptive fading rests on evidence from other domains.

**The loose style's numbers are thin.** They come from 56 finished works by one hand at 512 pixels, with no process evidence, covering four subject categories and effectively no people. The recommended stage order is designed, not observed.

**Dunn's method is known only from catalogue records and a review.** His books and videos were not read, so nothing about specific technique advice can be attributed to him, and the chapter sub-sections for the later chapters were not retrievable.

**All feedback thresholds are unvalidated.** No validated instrument for automated drawing-skill assessment was located, nor reliability figures for contour-distance scoring of learner art. Distance-to-reference metrics will be large and discouraging for children and must default to encouragement or be switched off.

### Licensing

**Several licences were confirmed only from secondary sources or prior knowledge** and need a primary-source read before shipping: SAM 2.1, EfficientSAM, Grounding DINO, RT-DETR, D-FINE, SDXL and SD 1.5, IP-Adapter, Florence-2, the MediaPipe framework, scikit-image, Shapely, the diffusion runtimes, and the weights of BiRefNet, Informative Drawings and Anime2Sketch. Open questions remain on the BIPED dataset behind TEED, the terms of the `lllyasviel/Annotators` checkpoints, and COCO-WholeBody behind the whole-body pose models. VTracer's MIT licence was read from the GitHub API, not the LICENSE file. The patent status of flow-based line-drawing techniques was not researched.

**Teaching content has its own boundary.** United States law excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems and methods from copyright ([17 U.S.C. 102](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec102.htm)), so techniques and generic exercise types are free to teach; tutorial wording, example artwork, traced diagrams and a course's distinctive exercise set and order are not. Loomis's books should be assumed protected: the claim that his family renewed the copyrights rests on secondary reports ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=31082)) and renewal records were not checked. Drawabox's licence could not be retrieved and should be assumed all rights reserved. The jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction status of older manuals was derived from general term rules, not retrieved sources. Write every instruction from the principle, generate all demonstration art in-house, and keep a provenance note per lesson.

**Named-artist styles.** Major platforms restrict named-living-artist prompts: OpenAI blocks individual living artists while permitting broader studio styles ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-blocks-artist-names-in-image-prompts-but-allows-studio-styles/)), and Adobe Firefly strips artist names, per its community forum ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-firefly-bugs/one-or-more-words-may-not-meet-user-guidelines-and-were-removed-when-i-enter-picasso-or-van-gogh/idi-p/14733199)). Both observations come from 2023–2025 reporting and forum posts and may have changed. The leading artists' case against image-model companies has no judgment on the merits; its summary-judgment hearing was moved to 17 February 2027 per a litigation tracker ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/02/03/sarah-andersens-copyright-lawsuit-the-1st-filed-v-ai-companies-gets-pushed-back-again-at-her-request/)), and whether its trial date held was not confirmed. The statement that style as such is unprotected reached this research through commentary on the Copyright Office's reports, not the reports themselves, and no case law on artist-name presets was retrieved. The risk in a named preset lies in publicity, endorsement and reputation more than in copyright. **Ship technique-named styles only.** The supplied sketch images belong to an artist whose bio offers the work for licensing; they are analysis evidence, must not be shipped, displayed or used as in-app examples, and should not be used as a default style reference.

**Uploaded images.** A lesson built from someone else's artwork is a potential derivative work. Add a rights attestation at upload, a takedown route, and keep such lessons private by default.

### Privacy

**All regulatory summaries come from law-firm and industry commentary, not primary texts, and none of this is legal advice.** The amended US children's privacy rule had a compliance deadline of 22 April 2026 and adds biometric identifiers such as facial templates to personal information, having dropped proposed language on data derived from facial data ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments)). The UK Children's Code sets 15 standards including high privacy by default and data minimisation ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/executive-summary/)). Whether an ordinary photo of a child counts as personal information under the US rule was not confirmed in retrieved sources. Apple's Kids Category wording is reported inconsistently by secondary trackers and the primary text was not fetched. EU consent ages, the EU AI Act, US state biometric laws and school-context rules were not researched. Legal review is needed before any face feature ships to children, and a data protection impact assessment should start early if the UK market or children are in scope.

### Product

**The window is open but not for long**: four small launches in a year. Automatic quality on arbitrary photos will set first impressions, so the product should state confidence per subject and start with categories it handles well. The teacher-tooling gap rests on a shallow search. Pricing anchors for family products sit near $6 per month or $50–65 per year, and paywall placement is the dominant complaint in the category. Machine-generated lesson imagery may not be copyrightable while teachers' edits may be, per a summary of Copyright Office guidance ([ECU summary](https://ai.ecu.edu/copy-ai-u-s-copyright-office-guidance/)), which affects how ownership of shared lessons is worded. Review volume for competing generators is too small to learn what users dislike about them.

## 9. Phased implementation roadmap — prove the core, then add styles, learners, models and the web

Durations are planning estimates for a small team and were not derived from any measurement.

| Phase | Deliverables | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Derivation spike** (1–2 weeks) | Apple and mug photos taken by script through mask, nested contours, primitive fitting and tonal regions into a hand-checked model file; four stages rendered to SVG; stability metrics computed; a one-week shell spike on stylus latency and canvas performance | Landmark drift exactly zero and silhouette gates met on both subjects; two or three people who draw rate the construction stage "as-is or minor edits"; a go or no-go on automatic part decomposition; a shell chosen |
| **1. Core prototype** (6–8 weeks) | Schema version 1 and zip container; template library of five to ten models; baseline photo pipeline with scribble segmentation; stage derivation; one style; teacher edits; templated text; step-sheet export; golden-file harness | Section 7 gates for stability, edit propagation, offline operation and failure handling pass on apple, mug and house; processing time measured on the reference machine |
| **2. Style system and full test set** (6–8 weeks) | The fourteen controls; structured and loose presets with organic and built sub-presets; seeded nested hatching and stippling; stroke-order playback; style-reference measurement; optional landmark provider for face and hand | Controllability gates pass 14 of 14; loose preset lands in its bands; all seven subjects run; blind rendering-quality rating meets the gate on primary cells |
| **3. Pilot with learners and teachers** (4–6 weeks) | Novice study on paper; teacher review sessions; edit counts and needs-review calibration; child-mode text and accessibility settings | Instructional-clarity gate met; teacher edits per lesson falling release to release; a decision on which subject categories to launch with |
| **4. Learner layer** (8–10 weeks) | Stylus capture; paper-photo rectification; drill modules with trend charts; measurement-based feedback with templated phrasing; adaptive fading ladder; portfolio benchmark drawings | Feedback thresholds calibrated on pilot data; agreement between automated observations and teacher judgement reported per metric; no whole-drawing score anywhere in the interface |
| **5. Optional local models** (4–6 weeks) | Small segmentation, depth and learned-edge providers behind the interface, each with a primary-source licence check and a measured size and latency | Each model shows a measured quality gain over the baseline on the golden set or is dropped; fallback verified |
| **6. Web build and sharing** (8–12 weeks) | Analysis ported to in-browser libraries; share-without-photo by link; teacher class distribution; rights attestation and takedown route; privacy review and impact assessment | Lesson files open identically on desktop and web; privacy and children's-design review complete with counsel |
| **7. Optional authoring-time AI** (later) | Language-model rewrite, translation and age adaptation of templated text, cached in the document; consent gate; adult-only | Product remains fully usable with the feature off; provider terms on minors checked at build time |

Phases 4 and 5 can run in parallel. The order after phase 3 should follow the pilot: if teachers are the keenest users, sharing moves ahead of the learner layer.

## 10. Immediate next step — a two-week derivation spike on an apple and a mug

**Run phase 0 now.** Take one apple photo and one mug photo through the deterministic pipeline by script: mask, nested contour simplification, envelope and ellipse fitting, part decomposition, nested tonal regions. Write the result into the proposed model format by hand where needed, render four stages to SVG, compute landmark drift, silhouette overlap and tone nesting, and put the construction and contour stages in front of two or three people who draw or teach. Add a dog photo as a stretch case.

This is the highest-value action because it attacks the only part of the plan with no precedent and no measurement. The representation, the staging logic, the style system, the licences and the product gap are each supported by published work or verified sources. Whether classical fitting yields construction shapes a teacher would accept is not, and every later phase assumes it does. Two weeks of work turns the report's largest estimate into a fact, produces the first real timing numbers, and settles the question that decides the product's scope: automatic lessons for any subject, or assisted authoring over category templates. Either answer leads to a product; they are different products, and the roadmap after phase 0 should be re-read in light of which one the spike supports.
